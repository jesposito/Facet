# What could you make with a Facet?

A Facet is a page made from your content. It can be a digital résumé for one recruiter, a proposal for one client, a collection of resources for an event, or a page in your website. You can share the same page directly and include it in your site navigation. Add or remove it from navigation whenever its job changes.

These are examples, not page types you have to choose between. You create and edit all of them under **Facets**.

## A résumé for a recruiter

Create a Facet with a useful name, such as “Learning designer résumé”. Give it a short address, choose the experience, projects and skills that fit the role, and write a headline and introduction for that audience. Per-item overrides let you tailor a summary on this page while keeping your original content for other pages.

Save it as **Unlisted** if you want to send a link without making the page public. Open its sharing section, generate a share link, and copy the revealed link. You can set an expiry or use limit. Check the link in a signed-out browser before sending it.

Anyone with that link can open the page and forward the link. It doesn't identify the recruiter or limit access to their email address. **Private** means only you can open it while signed in; it isn't a way to share with a recipient. For a page that asks visitors for a password, use **Password** instead.

Keep the full share link somewhere secure if you need it again. The page's bare address doesn't grant access to an unlisted Facet. Revoking a link stops it working; generating a replacement revokes the old link when the replacement is saved successfully. An expiry or use limit restricts future access, but can't recall anything a visitor already downloaded or copied.

## A proposal or resource page

For a proposal, gather relevant projects and approved testimonials, add custom content for the proposal itself, and choose a call to action such as a booking link. For an event, gather talks, posts and resources around the occasion. Choose the content and access that suit the people receiving the page.

Access controls protect the Facet itself. Content that is public on your homepage, in a feed or in another Facet stays public there. Review those pages and each item’s visibility when preparing a private résumé or proposal.

Your content can appear in several Facets. Editing the source updates the pages that include it; an override changes how that item appears in one Facet. Drafts stay out of visitor pages. Check the preview and test the actual visitor link before sharing.

## Pages in your website

You might build About, Work and Writing pages from the same content you use for individual recipients.

1. Create and save the page under **Facets**.
2. Set it to **Public** and keep it active when you're ready for anyone to open it.
3. Go to **Homepage → Site Navigation**. Enable navigation and turn on the page's entry. Adjust its label and order there.
4. Open your site while signed out and follow the navigation link.

Public, active Facets are eligible for site navigation. Making a page public and adding it to navigation are separate actions. A public page can still be shared by its direct address without being in the navigation.

A résumé you started as an unlisted page can join your site later: change its access to Public when you're ready, then enable its navigation entry. Its content and address stay with the same Facet. Removing a navigation entry doesn't make a public page private; change its access separately if that is your intention.

## Choose how each section gets its content

A section can show **All public content**, updating as you add eligible published content, or **Choose items**, using the items you select and their order. Switching between these choices keeps your manual picks. An empty manual selection shows nothing.

All public content never treats a retained private pick as public. Contacts follow their configured page availability, and testimonials must be approved and satisfy any list filter. If you explicitly select private content for a Facet, review that choice carefully: visitors who can open the Facet may then see that selected item.

Older Facets keep their selected items when opened or saved. Sections saved without an explicit content mode now use Choose items, so an old empty selection shows nothing. Review older pages and choose All public content where you want eligible content included automatically. New Facets use All public content for their enabled sections.

See [the features guide](FEATURES.md#visibility) for item visibility and [security and privacy](SECURITY.md) for access, sharing and storage details. Self-hosted Facet keeps its own data and configuration; these instructions don't describe Facet Cloud's editor or access settings.
